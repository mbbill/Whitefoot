Decision: A struct field may carry the modifier `readonly`, in any struct, whether the prelude or a source writer declares it, and the modifier states that the field is not assignable rather than that its value never changes: a path that ends at or passes through a readonly field is never the target of a `set` and is never the argument at a reference parameter whose callee row writes that parameter, construction gives such a field its value like every other field, a whole-value assignment replaces it together with its owner, and it otherwise changes only through an operation whose declared effect row carries `writes` of it, a declared row being free to name a readonly field in `writes` because a row reports every change its callees make, because the members the compiler maintains, a storage's length, its capacity and a ring's head, should then be ordinary fields of ordinary declarations rather than a list of members that only the specification's prose can state, and a writer with the same need, a member every holder may read that only the type's own operations change, can declare one for a type of their own, instead of compiler-maintained members reachable by no declaration form a writer could use.

Rejected:
- A different word for a field that is not assignable, chosen so that the spelling could not be read as a promise that the value never changes: rejected because `readonly` is the word most readers already arrive with, and a read-only member whose value does change under the owning type's own operations is the familiar meaning of that word rather than a surprise it creates.
