Decision: A loop containing a may-suspend operation gets a second, staged permission that cuts its body at the first submission into a prologue that always runs in index order and a remainder that may overlap a later prologue or another remainder, replicating storage only under a proved must-write and may-read coverage condition and throttling host-resource creation to the source-order footprint before any outcome publishes, because a writer-stated buffer extent on every effect row broke the very program the pipeline exists to accelerate while the derived footprint does not, and a pipelined I/O loop is the shape real programs need that neither the sibling-call window nor the counted-loop reduction can grant, instead of leaving every suspending loop sequential or requiring a hand-written pipeline of independent calls.
