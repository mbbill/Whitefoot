Decision: Every borrow holder has one immutable resolved root, binds once, and never rebinds or retargets at a control-flow merge, because banning borrow reassignment, which serves writer flexibility only, collapses the lval-set borrow types and retyping machinery a formal calculus otherwise needs down to singletons, and that collapse is the load-bearing simplification of the whole frontend-scale checker, instead of rebindable borrows.

Decision: A bounded family of reborrows is admitted, a statement-scoped child as a call argument, a mode-preserving returned reborrow of a parameter or let-bound holder, a borrow-mode match payload binder as an arm-scoped child, and a call-result holder whose provenance the signature alone determines, because strict no-reborrow forced the compiler's own source, at about a thousand sites that hand a borrow through to a callee, into threading owned values in place of borrows, a shape the language does not teach, while these bounded non-escaping forms preserve the reviewed no-alias facts, instead of strict no-reborrow.

Decision: A call result may be bound as a borrow holder only when the signature alone determines its provenance, exactly one parameter of the result's kind naming the result's region, because a summary derived from the callee's body would make callable contracts depend on implementations and need fixed points over recursive call groups, instead of body-derived provenance.

Rejected:
- Strict no-reborrow, with exclusive access flowing only by linear threading or restructuring: rejected because about a thousand sites that hand a borrow through to a callee forced the compiler into threading owned values in place of borrows, a shape the language does not teach, while the bounded child form preserves the no-alias facts; harder reborrow forms remain deferred rather than admitted.
