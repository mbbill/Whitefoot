Decision: Every borrow holder has one immutable resolved root, binds once, and never rebinds or retargets at a control-flow merge, because banning borrow reassignment, which serves writer flexibility only, collapses the lval-set borrow types and retyping machinery a formal calculus otherwise needs down to singletons, and that collapse is the load-bearing simplification of the whole frontend-scale checker, instead of rebindable borrows.

Decision: A bounded family of reborrows is admitted, a statement-scoped child as a call argument, a candidate-position child whose loan may survive in the bound call result and so suspends its parent for that value's lifetime with no statement-end resumption, a mode-preserving returned reborrow of a parameter or let-bound holder, a borrow-mode match payload binder as an arm-scoped child, a call-result holder whose provenance the signature alone determines, and shared or exclusive child views confined to proved subranges at no greater than their parent's loan strength, because strict no-reborrow forced the compiler's own source, at about a thousand sites that hand a borrow through to a callee, into threading owned values in place of borrows, a shape the language does not teach, while runtime-width rows and recursive output partitions additionally need view subdivision with explicit suspension of conflicting parent access and restoration when the child loans end, instead of strict no-reborrow. Child views retain the existing result-origin ceilings; forming an exclusive child locally does not widen a borrowed parameter's return authority.

Decision: A call result may be bound as a borrow holder only when the signature alone determines its provenance, exactly one parameter of the result's kind naming the result's region, because a summary derived from the callee's body would make callable contracts depend on implementations and need fixed points over recursive call groups, instead of body-derived provenance.

Decision: A completed owned-enum match header or exact owned-Boolean conditional header ends only the call-scoped temporary loans that header created, before the selected arm or branch, because retaining them for the whole statement blocked sequential typed acquisition through a retained unique provider after the owned header value had completed, while non-escape and completed-access checks permit the narrower endpoint without weakening exclusivity, instead of whole-statement retention of header loans.

Rejected:
- Strict no-reborrow, with exclusive access flowing only by linear threading or restructuring: rejected because about a thousand sites that hand a borrow through to a callee forced the compiler into threading owned values in place of borrows, a shape the language does not teach, while the bounded child form preserves the no-alias facts; forms outside the admitted family remain deferred.
- Shared-only child views through borrowed holders: rejected because they prevent recursive subdivision of exclusive output storage even when containment is proved and parent access is suspended, as the range consumers in research/investigations/range-loans/DESIGN.md demonstrate.
- Every header-created child reborrow living through the complete enclosing statement including every arm: rejected because it blocked sequential typed acquisition through a retained unique provider after the header value had completed.
