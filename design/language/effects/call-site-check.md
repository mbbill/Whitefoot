Decision: At a call the callee's declared row is substituted with the actual argument paths and the substituted effects are compared pairwise, a pair on overlapping paths of which at least one is a write being rejected unless the two paths are proved disjoint by different roots or by indices or ranges proved distinct, while read and read overlap is allowed, because one written row per callee plus a pairwise comparison at the call decides interference from signatures alone without reading any body, and an unproved pair is exactly the case a caller can repair by proving the disequality it already has, and because this node is new and replaces or retires nothing, the live tree recording the call-time projection of effect paths only inside `language/effects`'s exclusive-helper fact-kill decision and inside ownership's whole-place exclusivity, neither of which states the pairwise comparison of a substituted row that x1 Rule 10 makes the acceptance rule for a call, instead of a whole-argument exclusivity test, an alias analysis over callee bodies, or admitting an unproved pair and recovering at run time.

Decision: A by-value argument contributes the consumption of its place for a `move` and a read of its place for a copy to that same pairwise comparison, because an argument moved out of a place a second argument also reaches is the same interference as two writes even though a by-value parameter carries no row entry, instead of exempting by-value arguments from the comparison because nothing about them appears in the row.

Decision: The judgment that decides which paths overlap at a call is the single judgment used everywhere a path is compared with another, including reference invalidation and the permission for two adjacent statements to overlap in execution, because one conservative definition of prefix and overlap is what lets a caller's disequality proof serve all three consumers at once, instead of a separate interference test per consumer.
