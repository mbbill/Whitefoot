Decision: A `--par` module carries two lowerings of every function on a path from the entry to a handed-out call, the overlapped world and a sequential clone whose code is the sequential lowering byte for byte, with the default policy selecting one world once per process at bootstrap by whether a pool was asked for and neither world calling into the other, because the hand-out's rejoin takes the callee's result out of tail position and forecloses tail-recursion elimination, so no single lowering serves both worlds, measured as a 2.96 times pool-off tax that fell to 1.00 with the clone world, instead of one lowering with a per-task demand switch.

Decision: The clone set is the functions reachable from the entry that can reach a hand-out, derived from the call graph and the permission table, because only those functions have a lowering that differs between the two worlds, instead of cloning every function or marking clones by name.

Decision: `--par-sequential-refusal` enters the callee's existing sequential clone when a compute offer is refused and the callee cannot suspend, keeping the ordinary signatures, joins, and result ABI, adding no hidden parameters or runtime depth counters, and declining the compute offers beneath the clone it enters, because refusal reuses the failed-acquisition edge every offer already has and so adds none of the per-task demand query whose contention cost was rejected, instead of a runtime demand signal read on each call.

Decision: Under `--par`, every ordinary cyclic call-graph component gets one budget-carrying variant per member, the member's ordinary symbol keeping its signature and result ABI, taking an initial budget once per call into the component, and entering its variant, every intra-component call and published callback entering the callee's variant with one level less, and a variant handed nothing left entering its sequential clone, so the cut costs one compare and one branch per node above it and nothing below, while a component whose member may suspend, carries a staged completion pipeline, is a synthesized loop function, or has no sequential clone is left out and named with its reason in `--par-ledger`, as offer selection after checking that reads no acceptance path, because a recursion otherwise offers at every node at a per-node cost the leaf work does not pay for, measured as the quadrature kernel's wall ratio falling from 1.685 with no family to 1.031 at two lanes, from 1.564 to 0.987 at four, and from 1.665 to 1.000 at eight, with process CPU falling from 1.81 to 1.17 times the sequential control, instead of `N` private layers of ordinary parallel call levels or a global nesting bound.

Decision: The initial budget is the runtime's own answer, floor(log2(64 times the lane count)) clamped to 24, one lane's answer when no pool exists, and zero from the weak stub every module carries so that a scheduler-less link runs the sequential clone from the first node, with `--par-recursive-frontier auto|N|off` as the one control over that starting value, `auto` the default, `N` in 1..32 pinning it at compile time, and `off` emitting no family so every node offers, because the measured best fixed value differs between two and four lanes, so a number chosen at compile time is wrong at the other width, and 64 sequential leaves per lane is the measured constant that `sched/core.c` records beside the measurement that selected it, instead of a fixed compile-time depth.

Rejected:
- A per-task demand signal switching to a sequential clone per subtree when no idle capacity is reported: rejected because the signal cost contended read-modify-writes measured at 0.49 to 0.93 seconds on the fine-grain cell, while pool-off selection needs one decision per process.
- `--par-recursive-frontier N` as `N` private layers of ordinary parallel call levels before the sequential clones: rejected because defaulting it under plain `--par` failed its own acceptance on the four-CPU host at every depth tried, the best depth differing between two and four lanes, while one budget-carrying family reaches the same cut with one compare per node and takes its depth from the runtime.
