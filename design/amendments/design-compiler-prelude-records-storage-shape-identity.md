Node: compiler/prelude-records

Decision: The three [TYPE-9] storage shapes are declared as ordinary [PRE-1] opaque structs and resolution maps each of those declarations, and the cell's, onto the compiler-owned container identity every later stage already reads a written `Array`, `Slots`, `Ring` or `Box` through, because x1's [TYPE-2] declares all four in the prelude's own text while [TYPE-9] keeps their element storage, their omitted-capacity form and their [MSR-1] measure rows compiler-owned, so the declaration supplies the name, the refused constructor and the diagnostic ordinals while the identity supplies everything a struct body cannot state, instead of resolving a shape to its declaration like any other prelude struct, which would hand the checker a generic struct with one to three `u64` fields where it needs a window.

Decision: The compiler-owned nominal table keeps its three storage rows and the mapping from a spelling to an identity generalizes to cover all four, because the identities are dense ordinals read by type formation, layout, the measure rows and the release walk, so retiring the table would renumber every one of them for no change in accepted programs, while the one spelling-to-identity function is the single site where a prelude declaration becomes a container, instead of adding a fourth table row for the cell or keeping a separate cell-only flag, which are two mechanisms for one question.

Decision: The table no longer offers a nominal-type or constructor candidate of its own at name resolution, and the collision rank that compared a source declaration against it is deleted, because each of the four now contributes exactly those entries through its prelude declaration, so a second candidate from the table made every written `Array` ambiguous against its own declaration and a source `struct Array` collided with a table row rather than with the prelude record it actually collides with, instead of suppressing the declaration's entries and keeping the table's, which would leave [PRE-1]'s declaration preorder without the records its diagnostic ordinals are counted over.

Decision: A constructor `call` or destructuring `let_stmt` naming any of the four cites [TYPE-2], because x1 states that "a constructor `call` and a destructuring `let_stmt` naming any of the four is refused by [TYPE-2] like every opaque struct's", instead of keeping [TYPE-9] for the three shapes, which split one refusal across two rules for four declarations that are now the same kind of thing.

Decision: The prelude shape declarations use the ordinary optional capability bounds and lowercase const-parameter grammar, including an unconstrained `T` in `Box<T>`, because [GRAM-2] and [PROV-6] make an omitted bound impose no capability requirement and [TYPE-2] distinguishes its prose capacity `N` from a written lowercase const parameter, so these declarations need no prelude-only bound or naming rule, instead of restoring the retired mandatory `T: linear` spelling or interpreting the prose `N` as a source TYPEID parameter.

Rejected:
- Keeping the three shapes outside the prelude text as compiler-owned table rows: rejected because [PRE-1]'s declaration preorder begins with "each opaque struct above in written order with its refused constructor and its fields", the three shapes are now the first of those, and omitting them puts every later prelude diagnostic ordinal off by their records.
- Teaching the checker to read a shape's measures from the declared readonly fields rather than from [MSR-1]'s table: rejected because the table, not the declaration, is what gives a cell its *exact*, *bounded* or *absent* class and what distinguishes the constant-capacity from the runtime-capacity row of the same declaration.
