Node: compiler/storage-placement

Decision: A consumed aggregate may reuse a same-typed whole result or the storage of one consuming field of a complete struct result once the callee has captured its inputs, with the complete parent allocation and every other live storage keeping the ordinary interference checks, because [STOR-7] now decides in the language that any value may be relocated by copying its bytes, that no judgment depends on a value's address being stable, and that no accepted program can observe one, so where a result is placed is the compiler's choice at every value rather than a boundary a program could read, and the retained priority helper's redundant transfers of its entire inline run disappear under that choice, instead of allowing reuse only for whole results or promising same-place behavior in the language.

Decision: The field case is checked against [WIN-3]'s whole-owner consume rather than against surviving siblings and survives it unchanged, because a move out of a field now ends the owner, its other affine parts taking their compiler-derived release at that point [STOR-3], so at the moment the reuse is considered no sibling read of that owner is live and the live obligations are exactly the parent allocation's size and alignment and the storage of values other than the consumed owner, instead of withdrawing the field case because the language changed what a partial move does or keeping a sibling-read condition that can no longer be violated.

Rejected:
- Treating one consumed child as permission to reuse storage belonging to the complete result: rejected because the parent allocation's size and alignment remain live obligations, including when the returned child is smaller than its parent; the later sibling reads that were part of this reason are gone with [WIN-3]'s whole-owner consume and the size and alignment obligation alone now carries it.
- Keeping the reuse grounded only on the measured removal of redundant transfers and on leaving the source ownership boundary unchanged: rejected because that ground leaves the admissibility question to the boundary this implementation happens to keep, while [STOR-7] answers it in the language, the measurement then deciding only whether the reuse is worth taking rather than whether it is available.
