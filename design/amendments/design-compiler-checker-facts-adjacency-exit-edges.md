Decision: A statement whose normal continuation may leave its block — a `return_stmt`, a `give_stmt`, a `break_stmt`, or a `let_stmt` selecting `propagate_let_rhs` — is refused as a member of a [PAR-1] adjacency and ends the run it stands in, because [PAR-1] still promises that under a permitted overlap "bindings and every Whitefoot state place equal the source-order result" and a statement written after an exit-bearing one need not execute at all, so overlapping the two would produce observables source order does not, instead of reading the rule's dropped v0.59 sentence "every normal continuation of s1 reaches s2" as a deliberate widening and permitting the pair whenever its footprints are disjoint.

Decision: A statement carrying its own control flow and its own release edges — a `match_stmt`, a value initializer, a `loop`, and a `for_stmt` — is refused as a member of a [PAR-1] adjacency rather than judged on the footprint of its scrutinee or its header alone, because v0.60's [PAR-1] admits "two adjacent statements of one block" with no sentence putting an arm block outside the statement that owns it, so the statement's write paths include everything its arms write and a judgment that read only the scrutinee would omit them, and an omitted footprint element widens permission, instead of retaining v0.59's scrutinee-call member, whose admission rested on the sentence "its arm blocks are not statements of the enclosing block" that v0.60 no longer carries.

Rejected:
- Judging an exit-bearing statement on its footprint and permitting the adjacency when the footprints are disjoint: rejected because disjointness is not the property at issue; the later statement's execution is conditional on an edge the earlier statement may take, and no footprint comparison can establish that it runs.
- Refusing an exit-bearing statement only in the first position, on the ground that the later statement's own edge leaves after both have completed: rejected because the earlier statement is handed out under one of the two schedules and its lane is still live when the later statement takes its edge, which abandons an unjoined lane reading the caller's frame.
- Computing a `match_stmt`'s footprint as the union of its scrutinee and of every arm: rejected because an arm block's statements are judged by their own adjacencies, and folding them into one enclosing footprint would report a denial against a statement the writer cannot see from the reported site; the capability it buys is one scrutinee hand-out, which the run rule already reaches whenever the call is written as an ordinary `let`.
