Node: language/effects/call-site-check

Decision: A call substitutes actual argument paths into the callee's declared row and rejects a compared pair with a write unless the [shared path judgment](https://github.com/mbbill/Whitefoot/blob/main/design/language/ownership.md) proves the paths disjoint, the compared pairs being every two entries that different arguments supply and every two entries one argument supplies whose overlap depends on the values of their index or range positions, such as `values[i]` and `values[j]`; read/read overlap is allowed, because signatures and caller facts then determine interference without reading the body, while two entries one argument supplies that overlap whatever their positions are, such as `reads(p), writes(p.x)`, reach their storage through that one parameter, which the callee's body is checked against and the per-parameter `noalias` already covers, so the precise row is callable and a call kills only what its narrower write reaches, instead of whole-argument exclusivity, callee-body alias analysis, runtime recovery of unproved separation, or comparing every pair of one call's entries. This replaces the node's decision beginning "A call substitutes actual argument paths into the callee's declared row": that decision compared every pair, so a row reading a whole parameter and writing part of it was admitted at its declaration and refused at every call, leaving `writes(p)` as the only callable row for such a body; the owner selected this exemption for performance under safety, and the [one-argument rows investigation](../../research/investigations/one-argument-rows/DESIGN.md#soundness) argues its soundness for the callee, the caller, the backend and overlapping execution and records the probes.

Rejected:
- Comparing every pair of one call's entries, as the replaced decision did: rejected because two entries of one argument that overlap whatever their positions are can never be proved disjoint, so the comparison refuses every call of the precise row and forces `writes(p)`, which kills every caller fact about `p` and invalidates every reference into it, while protecting nothing that the callee's own check and the per-parameter `noalias` do not already protect.
- Refusing at the declaration every row with two overlapping entries on one parameter where one writes: rejected because it removes the dead end by forbidding the precise row, so a body that reads all of `p` and writes only `p.x` still declares `writes(p)` and its callers still lose every fact about `p`, which the owner's performance-under-safety ruling refused.
- Exempting every pair one argument supplies, value-dependent pairs included: rejected because the exemption is needed only where the comparison refuses every call, while a pair such as `reads(v[i]), writes(v[j])` is callable wherever the call proves its positions distinct, so exempting it removes no dead end, and dropping that call-site obligation is a separate question with no current evidence.
