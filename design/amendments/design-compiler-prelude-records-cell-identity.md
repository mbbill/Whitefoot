Decision: The cell `Box` is declared as an ordinary [PRE-1] opaque struct and resolution then maps that one declaration onto the compiler-owned container identity every later stage already reads a written `Box` through, because [TYPE-2] and [PRE-1] now declare the cell in the prelude's own text with a field and a refused constructor while [TYPE-9] keeps `Box<T>` a compiler-owned shape whose type formation, referent, layout and release the checker derives rather than reads off a struct body, instead of resolving `Box` to its declaration like any other prelude struct, which would hand the checker a generic one-field struct where it needs a cell and would need every `Box` judgment in type formation, storage, measure and release rewritten against a nominal template.

Decision: The three [TYPE-9] storage shapes keep their compiler-owned nominal table and the cell leaves it, holding its identity at the ordinal one past that table's end, because [TYPE-9] now says "three storage shapes, two placements each, and one cell" and the three are declared by no source item at all while the cell is declared by [PRE-1], so a table that still carried `Box` would offer it a second nominal-type and constructor entry beside its declaration and make a source `struct Box` collide with the table rather than with the prelude record it actually collides with, instead of keeping all four in one table and suppressing the cell's entries at each reader.

Decision: An opaque struct's constructor entry is an ordinary entry of the constructor TYPEID domain, in the resolved inventory and in the [PRE-1] diagnostic preorder alike, because [TYPE-2] states that the entry "exists to be refused" and sites the refusal as a hard error at the complete `call`, which is a judgment over a resolved declaration, instead of withholding the class at resolution, which made the refusal an unresolved-name rejection one stage early and left [TYPE-2]'s own diagnostic unreachable from source.

Decision: The cell's declaration is written `opaque struct Box<T: linear>` although [PRE-1]'s fence writes `opaque struct Box<T>`, because [GRAM-2]'s `gparam := TYPEID ":" (TYPEID | linearity_bound)` makes a bound mandatory and [FN-2] states that every type parameter of a nominal carries exactly one written bound with no default, so the fence as written parses at no point and stops every compilation in the prelude, and the widest linearity class is the one the same fence gives the same parameter of `box_new<T: linear>`, whose result is that cell, instead of treating the fence's bytes as authoritative over the grammar and leaving the prelude unable to declare itself.

Rejected:
- Giving the cell a declaration-backed nominal and teaching the checker to recognize it there: rejected because the checker's cell handling is keyed on the container identity at type formation, at the `inner` referent step, at layout, at the no-heap refusal and at the release walk, and the identity mapping preserves every one of those at one resolution site, while the alternative rewrites all of them for no change in accepted programs.
- Leaving `Box` out of the prelude text and keeping it a compiler-owned table row: rejected because [PRE-1]'s declaration preorder begins with "each opaque struct above in written order with its refused constructor and its fields", the cell is the first of those, and omitting it puts every later prelude diagnostic ordinal off by the cell's four records.
