Node: design/language/data-model/vector-consumption.md

Decision: A reusable Vector consumes a removed suffix synchronously through a monomorphized member in original order, preserving capacity and the retained prefix, by taking each first-half rear element into an owned local, exchanging it with the next suffix slot and consuming that local, then consuming the reversed remainder from the back, because the ordinary bounds and effect-overlap rules establish valid ownership and exclude callback observation of the backing, while the retained-record comparison with qualified independent stack groups and descriptor-before-transfer takes eliminates two transfers per pair compared with separate suffix reversal, instead of retaining the old separate reversal pass, walking the retained prefix or allocating scratch storage. Movement remains O(removed) with constant auxiliary element storage; displaced rear elements still move, so this is not a minimum-transfer or universal native-parity claim.
