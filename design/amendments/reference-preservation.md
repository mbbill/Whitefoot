Decision: Extend the existing reference-validity lattice with finite conjunctive preservation questions for potentially destructive call and assignment writes; copies, joins and the existing loop-header dependency closure retain every required target pair, and only a reference use submits those questions to the ordinary flow proof ledger at their original write sites, because REF-2 judges uses while OWN-7 requires the write's current proof context and an unused invalidated reference is legal, instead of unconditional EFF-5 separation obligations for bystanders, a function-wide separation cache, a second solver, or a mandatory structural replay after entailment. Assignment targets retain their pre-right-hand-side captures while preservation is judged at the post-right-hand-side commit. Each individual target pair retains the existing disjunction of admissible differing index or range positions; failure diagnoses the demanding use under REF-2 and does not suppress the event's effects. This supplements the current reference-validity and structural-checker/entailment handoff decisions without replacing their loop-header or current-ledger requirements.

Rejected:
- Rechecking every structural body after entailment supplies separation results: rejected because ordinary functions need no extra replay, while the existing replay ends before the complete function inventory is analyzed and therefore supplies neither the cross-phase identity contract nor the provenance needed by that additional pass.
- Demanding separation at every potential invalidation: rejected because a reference may legally become invalid when no later use depends on it.
- Proving preservation at the reference use: rejected because a later guard or postcondition cannot restore a validity fact removed by an earlier write.
