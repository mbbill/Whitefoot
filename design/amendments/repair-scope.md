Node: language

Decision: The specification fixes a rejection's rule, location and payload, states which rejections carry a repair, and requires of every alternative a repair names that, applied as written, it lets the rejected judgment succeed in a state that is not contradictory and writes nothing a rule rejects whatever the rest of the program is, leaving the words to the toolchain [DIAG-1], because a repair's words change no acceptance, because on main the prescribed and printed repairs differed at twenty-two of the forty-two reachable prescriptions while no check reads either, and because in the probes of the [repair-wording investigation](../../research/investigations/repair-wording/DESIGN.md#2-disposition-and-literal-application) the repairs that compiled in one round were the ones fitted to the case in hand, which a fixed sentence cannot name; that last ground is provisional, observed on minimal programs with one compiler, and reopens if the investigation's writer trial shows fixed words repairing faster, instead of prescribing each rule's repair sentence.

Rejected:
- Prescribing each rejection's repair words in the specification, amended per disposition: rejected because a fixed sentence cannot name the instance or select a route by the goal's terms, six reachable prescriptions are not printed at all and sixteen are printed in other words or with other remedies, and the writer-driven improvements to date landed in the compiler first; the [repair-wording investigation](../../research/investigations/repair-wording/DESIGN.md#1-inventory) holds the inventory.
- Prescribing each rejection's repair content per disposition while leaving the words free: rejected because the content varies by position and instance as well, the bare-affine repair having six printed variants where the specification states two, one of them avoiding the move out of an element that the specification's own repair would write, and the two conditions above exclude every failure the probes found without enumerating cases.
- Requiring only that a repaired construct is not rejected again by the same rule: rejected because it flags one of the seventeen failed literal applications in the probes, and only on the PR #123 compiler, while the others moved the rejection to another construct or rule or compiled the construct to dead code.
