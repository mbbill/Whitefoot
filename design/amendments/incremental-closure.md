Node: compiler/incremental-closure

Decision: A fact state records which part of its bound matrix is already its [ENT-4] closure, and a closure adds only the relations, terms and weakened selections changed since, falling back to the fixed point when that part is small, because the [incremental closure study](../../research/investigations/proof-certificate-architecture/INCREMENTAL-CLOSURE.md) measures the cubic closure recomputed at every kill, join and query as the dominant checking cost of real programs, and finds the incremental closure 21 to 28 times cheaper on them with bounds, disequalities and contradictions identical to the complete closure, instead of recomputing the complete closure at every closure point. The record tracks every kill and join, so it retires the refusals of such a cross-flow record in compiler/proof-query-context and compiler/closure-row-dominance.

Decision: When several derivations prove the same L0 bound, which one a state retains is left to the closure that found it, because [ENT-4] fixes derivability and dispositions but not that witness, and keeping the complete traversal's witness is what forced the cubic recomputation, instead of keeping every selected derivation identical to the complete traversal. A join therefore keeps a proof that all of its predecessors selected when none is contradictory. Ordinary fallback candidates are added only where the selected proof depends on a postcondition call. This retires the shared-promotion and shared-join closure refusals of compiler/closure-evaluation, which rested on keeping the derivation interning order.

Decision: The selected bounds of a fact state and every closed view are dense matrices, and the closed view of an unchanged state is remembered, because once closure is incremental the same study attributes the next costs to rebuilding hashed maps, sorting their keys and closing one unchanged state repeatedly, instead of hashed relation maps rebuilt at every closure, materialization and join.

Rejected:
- A second difference-bound implementation with its own kill rules: rejected because kills only remove cells and mark their terms for insertion, and the disequality and strengthening consequences that insertion settles are compared with the fixed point by the verification switch.
- Sharing matrix rows between the states that meet at a join: rejected because a kill or materialization in one arm rewrites a column in every row, so few rows would still be shared at the join.
- Storing only bounds stronger than their implicit path through zero: rejected because holder kills, joins and delivery would each need a separate argument for the omitted cells, and no measurement shows that the remaining cost needs it.
