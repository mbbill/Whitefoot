Node: compiler/incremental-closure

Decision: A fact state records whether its bound matrix is unknown, closed, or a closed core with fresh cells, fresh terms and weakened cells, for its full and its ordinary proof layers alike. A closure starts from that core: each fresh edge enters with one column pass and one row pass over the rows tight through the edge and the columns it can improve; up to one weakened cell per term is repaired in place within a bounded number of passes; any other core takes the seeded fixed point; and only a strictly smaller bound replaces a core cell. The reason is that the [incremental closure study](../../research/investigations/proof-certificate-architecture/INCREMENTAL-CLOSURE.md) measures the complete cubic fixed point, recomputed at every kill, join and query, as the dominant checking cost of real programs, and a test-only switch finds bounds, disequalities and contradictions identical to the complete closure across them. This is instead of recomputing the complete closure from all live facts at every closure point, or keeping a separate difference-bound implementation with its own kill and strengthening rules.

Decision: Which of several equal-bound L0 derivations a cell retains is an implementation choice. A join therefore keeps a proof every non-contradictory predecessor already selected, and an ordinary-fallback view adds candidates only where the selected proof depends on a postcondition call. The reason is that [ENT-4] fixes derivability and dispositions but not the equal-bound witness, and the retained-witness identity was what forced the complete traversal. This is instead of preserving the derivation identity of the complete traversal.

Decision: Selected bounds live in a dense, reference-counted store with stride slack, and only additional proof candidates sit in a side table. A closed state is a dense matrix, and the closed view of an unchanged state is remembered until a relation, a signed goal or the contradiction changes, keyed by the term, goal and derivation tables and their revisions. The reason is that, once the closure is incremental, the same study measures map rebuilding, key sorting and repeated closures of one state as the next costs. This is instead of hashed relation maps rebuilt at every closure, materialization and join.

Rejected:
- Sharing matrix rows between predecessor states so that joins visit only differing cells: rejected because a kill or materialization in one arm changes a column in every row, so few rows stay shared by the time they reach a join.
- Storing only cells stronger than their implicit path through zero: rejected because holder kills, joins and delivery images would each need their own argument for the omitted cells, and no measurement yet shows the remaining cost justifies it.
