Node: compiler/incremental-closure

Decision: A fact state records which part of its bound matrix is already its [ENT-4] closure, and closing the state inserts only the relations and terms added since and rederives the cells whose selected proof was removed with one of its candidates while both terms survive; it falls back to a fixed point seeded from the recorded closed part, which accepts only strictly smaller bounds and absent disequalities, when more cells were weakened than the state has terms or their rederivation does not settle, and to the complete unseeded fixed point when nothing is recorded as closed or the closure leaves out a term, because the [incremental closure study](../../research/investigations/proof-certificate-architecture/INCREMENTAL-CLOSURE.md) measures the cubic closure recomputed at every kill, join and query as the dominant checking cost of real programs, and with this node's three decisions together finds its two largest real programs checked 21 to 28 times faster and three smaller ones 4 to 9 times faster, with bounds, disequalities and contradictions identical to the complete closure, instead of recomputing the complete closure at every closure point. The record tracks every kill and join, so it retires the refusals of a cross-flow closure cache in compiler/proof-query-context and compiler/closure-row-dominance.

Decision: When several derivations prove the same L0 bound, which one a state retains is left to the closure that found it, because [ENT-4] fixes derivability and dispositions but not that witness, and keeping the complete traversal's witness is what forced the cubic recomputation, instead of keeping every selected derivation identical to the complete traversal. A join therefore keeps a proof that all of its predecessors selected when none is contradictory, and the ordinary view, which excludes proofs that use a callee's postcondition, receives its own candidates only for cells whose selected proof uses one. This retires compiler/closure-row-dominance's refusal of replacing the witness-selection algorithm, which rested on keeping that witness.

Decision: The selected bounds of a fact state and every closed view are dense matrices, and the closed view of an unchanged state is remembered, because once closure is incremental the same study attributes the next costs to rebuilding hashed maps, sorting their keys and closing one unchanged state repeatedly, instead of hashed bound maps rebuilt at every closure, materialization and join. The remembered view withdraws two refusals proposed earlier in the compiler/closure-evaluation amendment: sharing one closure between a kill batch's contradiction promotion and its materialization, refused for a measured 1.04x gain through a separate promotion path, which the remembered view provides without one; and sharing predecessor closures between join promotion and the join, refused because it reordered derivation interning, which the previous decision no longer fixes.

Rejected:
- A second difference-bound implementation with its own kill rules: rejected because a term kill only removes its terms' cells and marks those terms for insertion and a candidate removal only marks the cells it weakens, and a test-only check, run in the gate on a real program and on generated flows, recomputes each incremental closure from all live facts and requires the rederived cells and the disequality and strengthening consequences of insertion to match the complete closure.
- Sharing matrix rows between the states that meet at a join: rejected because a kill or materialization in one arm rewrites a column in every row, so few rows would still be shared at the join.
- Storing only bounds stronger than the bound their terms' type ranges imply through zero: rejected because removing postcondition proofs at a kill, joining states and delivering relations to a receiver would each need a separate argument for the omitted cells, and no measurement shows that the remaining cost needs it.
