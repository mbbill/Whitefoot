- Parallel permission derives from checked ownership, effects, dataflow, control flow, and domain proofs. Permission failure preserves sequential behavior rather than rejecting otherwise valid source.
- Proof-only contracts and invariants introduce no scheduling edge. The old false-claim trap semantics are retired; observable program behavior follows the active PAR and system rules.
- Permission and actualization are separate. compiler/README.md owns the current default completion path, --par compute behavior, --no-overlap reference, and target limits.
- Optional optimizer facts cannot change acceptance or supply a separate parallel proof authority. Runtime scheduling must preserve the language's observable behavior.
- Diagnostic reports explain judgments and actualization but do not grant proof authority. The detailed current window and loop rules live in the specification, not the dated v1 designs below.

- 2026-08-23 (5d73ae59) statement: the 2026-08-21 entry above no longer describes the tree. Its premise — eligibility limited to claim-free closures — was withdrawn, and its conclusion holds only in part: no arbitration machinery and no coordinator is built, but a parked lane is. A module emits the trap latch exactly when it both carries a `claim` and hands a call out to a worker lane; the default build and an overlap-capable build that actualizes nothing emit the trap path they emitted before the latch existed. What parks is a losing thread of an already-erroneous execution, and the winner's abort takes the process down with it. (code)
- 2026-08-23 (f6c55a9d) pitfall: emitting the latch on the sole condition that a module carries a `claim` changed the bytes of 21 of the corpus's 39 default compilations and built a parked lane into a world where a lone thread can never race anyone for the record; the condition an erroneous execution needs is two threads, not a claim. (code)
- 2026-09-06 statement: one scheduler core (`compiler/src/backend/sched/`) replaces both parallel runtimes and both writer schedulers; a join on I/O parks the stack and the reaper publishes through the same core, every operation lowers as one submit-then-join through a record in the submitting frame, and the design's schedules are enumerated under `completion-test`. The floor entry runs on a pool stack, so a program with no lanes and a program with eight are one runtime. Evidence: `research/experiments/park-on-miss-switch-cost/` (one stack switch is about one percent of a park-and-wake) and `research/experiments/park-on-miss-measurements/` (every §12 bar of the design with its number). (sourced)
- 2026-09-06 measurement: the price of parking at a compute miss, measured at landing against `main` at 82f6d6a with each tree's own compiler on `par_layout`: within two percent at one and two workers, eight to nine percent behind at four and eight (0.409 s against 0.446 s at four, 0.431 against 0.464 at eight, medians of five alternating runs), the narrowed form of the compute-miss item `research/experiments/park-on-miss-measurements/README.md` §2 leaves open; the idle spin after the drain is what narrowed it from 45 and 103 percent. Programs whose loop split hands out large chunks pay nothing. (sourced)
- 2026-09-10 (6816e9bd) boundary: the two 2026-09-06 entries describe the superseded park-on-miss scheduler and its measurements, not the current runtime. Compute joins now execute and help tasks on ordinary native thread stacks; I/O retains submit-then-join with the caller waiting on its own stack independently of compute helping. There is no switchable-stack pool, and the managed-stack enumerator is retired.
